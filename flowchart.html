StadiumStart or end
RectangleAn action
DiamondA decision

CylinderWritten to a table
Double-barredRuns another procedure

01 Leave request and approval

Three people touch this: the employee who asks, the manager who endorses, and HR who decides. Nothing affects pay until the last diamond says yes.

The step people forget. Approval alone changes nothing a payslip can see. The attendance calculation has to run again over those dates before the absence disappears — which is why a late approval sometimes shows up as a deduction that is already paid.

02 What one day becomes

The attendance calculation asks these questions in this order, for every employee on every date in the range. The first yes wins — which is why a public holiday beats a missing scan.

Order is the whole design. A day that is both a public holiday and has no scan is a holiday, not an absence, because the holiday question is asked first. Move the questions around and the same scans produce different pay.

03 Running payroll for a month

Opened for one branch and one period. The diamonds are the checks that stop a run producing nonsense, and the settings that change what comes off a payslip.

04 Requisition to hire

Four gates between a vacancy and a new employee. Fail any of them and the candidate record stays, marked rejected — which is what makes the blacklist and the re-apply check possible later.

Drawn from the live HRMS schema and the deployed procedures. Shapes follow standard flowchart notation — stadium for start and end, rectangle for an action, diamond for a decision, parallelogram for input and output, cylinder for a table written to, double-barred rectangle for another procedure being called.
