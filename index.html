04 Recruitment

A vacancy is raised, applicants attach to it, each sits an exam built from the shared question bank, and the interview result decides the offer.

the question bank is shared with Engagement surveys
Worth knowing. ENG.QUESTIONNAIRE serves two masters. Rows typed EXAM feed recruitment examinations; rows typed SURVEY feed engagement surveys. They share one table and one editor, and each side filters to its own type — which is why the two have separate question-pool procedures.

05 Time & attendance

Raw scans go in at one end and a judged day comes out at the other. Four different kinds of request can change what a day means after the fact.

Why the calculation is re-runnable. ATT.ATTENDANCE is derived, never typed. Approve a leave request for last Tuesday and nothing changes until the calculation runs again for that date — at which point Tuesday stops being an absence. The same is true of a holiday added late, or a scan appended by hand.

06 Payroll — the monthly cycle

A run is opened for one branch and one month. Everything else is read, not entered: the contract says what someone earns, attendance says what they actually worked, and the setting tables say what is taken off.

07 Inside the payroll engine

The order matters: tax is worked out on what is left after NSSF, so NSSF has to be settled first. Each step writes rows into the payslip, and the header totals are summed from those rows at the end.

Resolve the run

Branch and period are required. Pick the exchange rate that applies — the whole calculation is done in riel, because the tax and NSSF bands are.

Build the employee set

Everyone with an active contract carrying a salary above zero, in that branch. A contract that starts or ends mid-month is pro-rated from its own dates.

Fold in attendance

Scheduled days, present, absent, leave, late minutes, early minutes, overtime hours, public holiday and day-off work — all read from ATT.ATTENDANCE.

Earnings

Basic, divided by the contract's own working days per month rather than a fixed figure, plus overtime, holiday and day-off pay, plus rule-driven allowances.

Attendance deductions

Absence by the day, late and early by the minute. A day already counted as absent contributes no late or early minutes — that double count was a real bug.

NSSF by wage band

The riel wage picks a band. Pension splits employee / employer; health and occupational risk are employer-only. A company setting can move the employee's half onto the employer, in which case nothing is deducted.

Tax on salary

Taxable earnings, less family relief, less the employee's NSSF where the setting allows it, then run down the bands. Residents and non-residents use different tables.

Seniority and advances

Length-of-service pay where it is due, and any salary advance already handed out this period comes back off the net.

Write it out

Delete the previous result for this run and insert fresh headers and lines. Pay currency and per-employee pay method chosen on the screen survive the rewrite.

Re-running is safe and expected. The procedure clears its own previous output for that run before writing. It does not clear anything a human chose on the screen — pay currency and cash-or-bank are carried across deliberately.

08 The request and approval chain

Four modules share one workflow — leave, working/overtime, late-early excuse, and asset requests. Learning the chain once covers all of them.

RequestTableWhat approval changes
LeaveATT.LEAVE_REQUEST The days stop being absences and come off the annual leave balance.
Working / overtimeATT.WORKINGREQUEST Hours become payable overtime, or a day off becomes a paid working day.
Late / earlyATT.LATEARLYREQUEST Those minutes stop being deducted from pay.
AssetASS.ASSET_REQUEST An assignment can be raised against the asset and it leaves the pool.
Nothing counts until it is approved. Every one of these is read by the attendance calculation with a status filter. A request sitting at SUBMIT has no effect on anyone's pay — which is the point, but it also means a forgotten approval looks exactly like an absence.

10 Patterns that repeat everywhere

Five conventions account for most of the schema. Once you recognise them, a table you have never opened is already half familiar.

PatternHow you spot itWhat it is for
Audit twin X and X_STM — 78 pairs Every save writes a row to the twin holding the record before and after as JSON, with who, when, from which machine. The History tab on every screen reads it.
Soft delete LTR_STS = NORMAL / DELETE Nothing is ever really removed. With 506 foreign keys pointing inward a hard delete would be impossible anyway, and the audit trail has to survive.
Procedure set SPV_ SPI_ SPL_ SP_*_B4SAVE SP_*_SAVE List, read one, droplist, validate, write. B4SAVE and B4DELETE only ever return a verdict and a sentence — they change nothing, so a refusal reads as plain English rather than a SQL error.
Parameter list DSP.PARAMET_LIST keyed by PML_TYPE Every droplist in the product. Tables store the code, never the id, so renaming a label is safe and translating it costs nothing.
Generated codes PML_TYPE = 'CODE_GEN' Prefix and digit count per record type, so EMP-0001, CRS-0001, EXAM-032 all come from one place and none of them is typed by hand.

Where the words come from

No label is hard-coded in a screen. SYSUP.SYSLANGUAGE holds one row per form per language, each a JSON array of caption keys — which is why the whole product switches between English and Khmer without a rebuild.
