RecognitionThe positive counterpart to the leaderboard. Anyone can give it, which is what keeps it from feeling like management theatre.
One design decision worth agreeing before it is built. A public "most late" ranking names and shames, and the people on it are often the ones with the longest commute or a child to drop off. The screens above split it: early birds are public, because being praised in front of colleagues is the point — and lateness is private, shown only to you and your manager, where it leads to an excuse request instead of a joke in the group chat. Same data either way. Say if you want it public and I will draw that version.

05 The screen map

Twenty-two screens behind five tabs. Everything a member of staff can reach in at most two taps from opening the app.

Three ways to clock in, on purpose. No single method works everywhere. GPS drifts indoors and in a concrete branch can put someone across the street. QR needs the code on the wall but is exact, and rotating it every minute means a photographed code is worthless. Face ID needs nothing on the wall at all and is the only one that proves it was that person rather than their phone — which is the thing a clock-in is actually claiming. Offer all three and let each branch use what suits it.

What is deliberately not here. No chat — people already have one. No document upload on a phone. No payroll run, no approving a payroll, no editing anyone's record: those are desk jobs with consequences, and they belong on the web app where there is room to see what you are doing.
